# scam-signal report

**Verdict:** Very likely a scam  
**Risk:** VERY HIGH · **Confidence:** high · **Channel:** sms

## Likely type: Bank / fraud-department impersonation

Someone posing as your bank's fraud team gets you to move money to a 'safe account', read back a one-time code, or 'reverse' a fake charge, often via Zelle.

- FTC Consumer Sentinel: Imposter scams: business imposter (bank)
- FBI IC3: Government/Business Impersonation; Phishing/Spoofing
- CFPB: Fraud or scam (Money transfer, virtual currency, or money service / Checking or savings account)
- Authorized-vs-unauthorized: scam (you would be authorizing a payment or action under deception)

## Red flags (strongest first)

1. **Threatens arrest, account closure, fines or other consequences**: "Your account will be locked in 30 minutes."
2. **Creates urgency or a short deadline**: "Your account will be locked in 30 minutes."
3. **Claims to be a bank, government agency or well-known company**: "If NOT, call our fraud department immediately at 555-0142."
4. **Tells you to call a phone number given in the message**: "If NOT, call our fraud department immediately at 555-0142."

## What to do now

- Don't reply, click links, scan QR codes, or call numbers in the message.
- Verify independently: open the official app, or call the number on the back of your card / on the official website you type in yourself.
- Don't send money, gift cards or crypto, and never share one-time codes, passwords or your SSN with anyone who contacted you.
- Keep the evidence (screenshot it), then block the sender and report it (see below).
- Your real bank will never ask you to move money to a 'safe account', read back a code, or send a Zelle payment to yourself or to 'reverse' a charge.
- Hang up and call the number on the back of your card.

## If you already responded, paid or shared info

- Sent money by bank/Zelle/wire: call your bank's fraud line immediately and ask them to stop or recall it. Speed matters.
- Paid with gift cards: contact the card brand right away with the card numbers and receipt.
- Sent crypto: report to the exchange you used and to IC3 with wallet addresses and transaction IDs.
- Shared a password or code: change it, enable 2FA, and sign out other devices.
- Shared your SSN or ID: go to IdentityTheft.gov and freeze your credit.
- Beware 'recovery' services that contact you promising to get the money back. That is a second scam.

## Where to report

- Forward the text to 7726 (SPAM) to report it to your carrier; then use 'Report Junk' and delete
- Your bank or card issuer: use the number on the back of your card or in the official app, never one from the message
- FTC (all U.S. scams): https://reportfraud.ftc.gov
- CFPB (if your bank/app won't help after a fraud or scam): https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/
- Robocalls/spoofed caller ID: FCC: https://consumercomplaints.fcc.gov
