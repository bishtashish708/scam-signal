# scam-signal report

**Verdict:** Very likely a scam  
**Risk:** VERY HIGH · **Confidence:** high · **Channel:** email

## Likely type: Account credential phishing

A 'verify your account / unusual sign-in / mailbox full / shared document' message that leads to a fake login page.

- FTC Consumer Sentinel: Imposter scams: business imposter
- FBI IC3: Phishing/Spoofing
- CFPB: Fraud or scam
- Authorized-vs-unauthorized: scam (you would be authorizing a payment or action under deception)

## Red flags (strongest first)

1. **Link paypa1-resolution.com: lookalike domain**: "paypa1-resolution.com imitates paypal.com (character substitution)."
2. **Asks for passwords, PINs, SSN, card or account details**: "Please confirm your card details within 24 hours."
3. **Email: dmarc fail**: "DMARC failed: the message is not verified as coming from paypa1-resolution.com."
4. **Email: display name impersonation**: "Display name 'PayPal Service' suggests paypal, but it was sent from an unrelated domain (paypa1-resolution.com)."
5. **Email: sender lookalike domain**: "Sender domain: paypa1-resolution.com imitates paypal.com (character substitution)."
6. **Email: risky attachment**: "Attachment 'Secure_Message.html' is a type commonly used to deliver phishing pages or malware. Don't open it."
7. **Email: link text mismatch**: "Link text shows www.paypal.com but actually goes to paypa1-resolution.com."
8. **Threatens arrest, account closure, fines or other consequences**: "Your account has been restricted."
9. **Email: reply to mismatch**: "Replies go to claims@resolutioncentre-help.net, not the sender's domain paypa1-resolution.com."
10. **Creates urgency or a short deadline**: "Please confirm your card details within 24 hours."

## What to do now

- Don't reply, click links, scan QR codes, or call numbers in the message.
- Verify independently: open the official app, or call the number on the back of your card / on the official website you type in yourself.
- Don't send money, gift cards or crypto, and never share one-time codes, passwords or your SSN with anyone who contacted you.
- Keep the evidence (screenshot it), then block the sender and report it (see below).
- Go to the service directly (not via the link) to check for alerts.
- If you entered a password: change it everywhere you reused it, turn on two-factor authentication, and sign out other sessions.

## If you already responded, paid or shared info

- Sent money by bank/Zelle/wire: call your bank's fraud line immediately and ask them to stop or recall it. Speed matters.
- Paid with gift cards: contact the card brand right away with the card numbers and receipt.
- Sent crypto: report to the exchange you used and to IC3 with wallet addresses and transaction IDs.
- Shared a password or code: change it, enable 2FA, and sign out other devices.
- Shared your SSN or ID: go to IdentityTheft.gov and freeze your credit.
- Beware 'recovery' services that contact you promising to get the money back. That is a second scam.

## Where to report

- Forward phishing email to reportphishing@apwg.org and use your mail app's 'Report phishing' button
- FTC (all U.S. scams): https://reportfraud.ftc.gov
- FBI IC3 (internet, crypto, investment, extortion, large losses): https://www.ic3.gov
